B. COSMETIC COMPLIANCE (FDA / MoCRA / EPA / CA) — verified

  • MoCRA small-business exemption: avg annual US cosmetic gross sales < $1,000,000 (inflation-adjusted; FDA has posted no higher figure) → exempt from facility registration, product listing, AND GMP. New facilities otherwise register within 60 days; new products list within 120 days; listings update annually; biennial renewals running through 2026.
  • CARVE-OUT from the exemption (must register/list even if small): products that (1) regularly contact the mucous membrane of the eye, (2) are injected, (3) are for internal use, (4) alter appearance >24 hrs. → Eye "repair" cream and lash products near the eye, and oral rinse/tooth products (internal use), likely lose the exemption — register/list them.
  • Serious adverse event: Responsible Person must report to FDA within 15 business days (with retail label); keep records 6 years (3 for qualifying small biz). Label must carry a US contact (address/phone/electronic) to receive adverse-event reports.
  • Safety substantiation (§608): keep records showing "adequate substantiation of safety." No specific test mandated; animal testing not required.
  • Cosmetic-vs-drug line: an intended-use/therapeutic claim makes a cosmetic an unapproved new drug. Tallow is not a monograph active, so therapeutic claims on it are illegal regardless of truth. FDA is actively enforcing (Dr. Brite warning letter 2024).
  • EPA insect repellent (FIFRA): repelling insects = pesticidal claim = EPA registration UNLESS the product meets all 6 conditions of the 25(b) minimum-risk exemption (40 CFR 152.25(f)) — only listed actives (cedarwood, citronella, geraniol, lemongrass, peppermint, rosemary, thyme oils, etc.) + permitted inerts (edible fats/oils are eligible inerts), full ingredient label, producer contact, no safety claims, no "EPA approved," and NO disease linkage (may say "repels mosquitoes/ticks" but NOT "ticks that carry Lyme"). EPA is actively considering removing personal mosquito/tick repellents from 25(b) — tail risk.
  • Pet products: "grooming aids" (cleanse/beautify only) are largely unregulated by FDA; a health/treatment claim makes it an unapproved animal drug. Topical pet repellent = EPA (FIFRA/25(b)); oral = FDA CVM. Safest: grooming balm, zero pest/health language.
  • Labeling (21 CFR 701 + FPLA): identity on PDP; net quantity dual US+metric "Net wt 4 oz (113 g)" (FTC 16 CFR 500.8); name+place of business ("Manufactured for/Distributed by" if co-packed); INCI ingredients in descending order (<1% any order after); penalty warning "Warning—The safety of this product has not been determined" required ONLY if safety not substantiated → substantiate to avoid it. OTC drugs need a Drug Facts panel (21 CFR 201.66) + monograph compliance.
  • California: Prop 65 (warn for listed chemicals over safe-harbor; short-form must now name a chemical; plain tallow balm w/ no powders/added retinoids/lead-tested colorants likely no trigger). CA Safe Cosmetics Act reporting only if ≥$1M sales. Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act (AB 2762): 24 banned ingredients in force since 1/1/2025; AB 2771 bans intentionally-added PFAS; AB 496 bans 26 more effective 1/1/2027 (reformulate before then). Tallow itself unlisted; risk lives in preservatives/fragrance/colorants.

In CA: CDPH Processed Food Registration (no small-biz exemption) and/or CDFA meat licensing; NOT cottage-food eligible; must be physically separated from cosmetics. Label Compliance Checklist checklist

Cosmetic label compliance checklist (MoCRA-aware).

Per-Product Label Compliance Checklist

This is regulatory research, not legal advice. Have counsel review labels and claims before printing or opening.

Run this for every product before printing labels. Pair it with the claim review — the label cannot carry a claim that isn't green in claim_tracker.csv.

A. FDA / FPLA required label elements (every cosmetic)

  • [ ] Identity of the product on the PDP (principal display panel) — what the product is.
  • [ ] Net quantity of contents, dual US + metric, on the PDP: e.g. "Net wt 4 oz (113 g)" (FTC 16 CFR 500.8).
  • [ ] Name + place of business of the manufacturer, packer, or distributor. If co-packed, use "Manufactured for ___" or "Distributed by ___."
  • [ ] Ingredient declaration (INCI), in descending order of predominance; ingredients <1% may be listed in any order after the 1%+ ingredients.
  • [ ] Penalty/safety warning — "Warning—The safety of this product has not been determined" is required ONLY if safety is not substantiated. → Substantiate safety (§608) so you don't have to carry it. Keep the substantiation records on file.

B. OTC drug elements (ONLY if a drug claim is intentionally kept)

If a product is built as an OTC drug (e.g. pain or vapor product with a monograph active):

  • [ ] Drug Facts panel (21 CFR 201.66) present and correctly formatted.
  • [ ] Monograph compliance — the active(s), strength, and indications match an OTC monograph (e.g. menthol/camphor for pain; menthol/camphor/eucalyptus for vapor).
  • [ ] If you are NOT building it as a drug → remove the drug claim (see claim_review_sop.md) and this section does not apply.

C. MoCRA

  • [ ] Small-business exemption — if avg annual US cosmetic gross sales < $1,000,000 (inflation- adjusted; FDA has posted no higher figure), exempt from facility registration, product listing, and GMP.
  • [ ] CARVE-OUT — exemption is LOST for products that (1) regularly contact the eye mucous membrane, (2) are injected, (3) are for internal use, or (4) alter appearance >24 hrs. → Eye "repair" cream, lash products near the eye, and oral rinse / tooth products must be registered (facility) and listed (product) even though sales are small.
  • [ ] Responsible-person US contact on the label (address / phone / electronic) to receive adverse-event reports.
  • [ ] Serious adverse event report to FDA within 15 business days (with retail label); keep records 6 years (3 for qualifying small biz).
  • [ ] Safety substantiation records (§608) kept on file for each product.
  • [ ] New facilities register within 60 days; new products list within 120 days; listings update annually; biennial renewals running through 2026.

D. California bans & warnings

  • [ ] Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act (AB 2762) — 24 banned ingredients in force since 1/1/2025. Confirm no banned ingredient is present.
  • [ ] AB 2771 — bans intentionally-added PFAS in cosmetics. Confirm none.
  • [ ] AB 496 — bans 26 more ingredients effective 1/1/2027. Reformulate before then if any appear in your formulas.
  • [ ] Prop 65 — warn for listed chemicals over the safe-harbor level (short-form must now name a chemical). Plain tallow balm with no powders / added retinoids / lead-tested colorants likely has no trigger. Risk lives in preservatives, fragrance, and colorants — check those.
  • [ ] CA Safe Cosmetics Act reporting — only required if ≥ $1M sales. (Not triggered below.)

Tallow itself is unlisted/low-risk across these. The CA risk lives in preservatives, fragrance, and colorants — review those inputs against AB 2762 / AB 496 / Prop 65.

E. Water-containing products (formulation gate that affects the label)

  • [ ] Anhydrous (water-free) balms — typically preservative-free, antioxidant only (vitamin E / rosemary extract). Any shelf-life date claim must be substantiated.
  • [ ] Any water-containing product REQUIRES a broad-spectrum preservative + a Preservative Efficacy Test (USP <51>) before it can be labeled and sold — non-negotiable.

Before printing labels — the gate

Do not send any label to print until: (1) the product's claim_tracker.csv row is green (no un-reviewed claim on the label), (2) MoCRA carve-out resolved and the responsible-person US contact is on the label, (3) CA ingredient bans cleared, and (4) counsel has reviewed the final label artwork.

Batch-to-Shelf SOP

This is the daily runbook for turning raw materials into released, labeled, sellable units. The Control Room handles production data + COGS + lot traceability (a workflow layer, $0); Shopify stays the system of record for sellable inventory and sales. The accounting ledger of record stays in QuickBooks — the Control Room's unit COGS feeds QBO, it does not replace it.

GMP discipline (RESEARCH_FACTS C — keep these distinct)

  • Master Manufacturing Record (MMR): the template/recipe, version-controlled. Lives in the Control Room as a Recipe (Recipes + Recipe_Items tabs). One per product. Change it deliberately and note the version.
  • Batch Production Record (BPR): the executed evidence for one specific batch — materials + lots weighed, equipment, temp/time, in-process checks, operator initials, deviations. Captured per batch in the Batches row + the append-only Material_Ledger.
  • Retention samples: keep a labeled retain of each lot (rotate oldest tallow first to manage rancidity; this scopes any recall to the affected batches).
  • Costing model: weighted-average material cost (defensible for blended handmade goods) + current-lot traceability for physical recall scope. True multi-lot FIFO cost layering is out of scope by design — fine at this single-site handmade scale.

Step 1 — Receive materials (menu: 🏭 Production → 📥 Receive materials)

Do this whenever stock arrives, before you make anything from it.

  1. Open 🛠 Frontier Control Room → 🏭 Production → 📥 Receive materials.
  2. Enter material, type (Raw material / Packaging), unit, vendor, lot ref, qty, and the lot's unit cost.
  3. The system: - Adds the qty to Materials.Qty on hand. - Re-computes the weighted-average Unit cost across what was already on hand + the new receipt. - Sets Current lot ref to the new lot and stamps Last received. - Writes a + Receipt row to the Material_Ledger (append-only audit trail).
  4. Supplier qualification (RESEARCH_FACTS C): require a CoA per lot; test the first 3–5 lots vs CoA; keep a vendor/lot record. No payment/bank IDs in the sheet.

Qty on hand turns red at/below Reorder point. Reorder point = (avg daily usage × avg lead time) + safety stock; recalc every 3–4 months.

Step 2 — Make a batch (menu: 🏭 Production → 🧪 Make a batch (recipe → COGS))

This is the costed path. (Use ➕ Quick batch only for fast entry without costing.)

  1. Make sure the product has an active Recipe (Recipes header + Recipe_Items lines). Run 🧷 Seed a production EXAMPLE first if you want to test, then delete the demo.
  2. Open 🧪 Make a batch (recipe → COGS); pick the recipe, enter make date, maker, yield qty, rejects.
  3. The system: - Consumes each recipe material from Materials (decrements Qty on hand). - Logs each draw as a − Consume row in Material_Ledger with the lot ref and running balance — this is your traceability. - Computes Batch material cost = Σ(qty × weighted-avg unit cost), Packaging cost, and Unit COGS = (material + packaging) ÷ (yield − rejects). - Generates the batch ID in the form FO-YYYYMMDD-PRODUCTCODE-### (e.g. FO-20260622-PAINBALM-001).
  4. Confirm the BPR essentials are captured in the Batches row: materials/lot ref, maker, yield, rejects, recipe ID, deviations in Notes.

Step 3 — Hold / QA

  1. New batches default to Release status = Hold.
  2. Run QA: in-process checks, visual/scent, label proof, and pull the retention sample for the lot.
  3. Water-containing products require a broad-spectrum preservative + Preservative Efficacy Test (USP <51>) — do not release a water-containing batch without it (RESEARCH_FACTS C). Anhydrous balms can ship preservative-free with antioxidant (vitamin E / rosemary extract).

Step 4 — Release

  1. When QA passes, set Release status = Released on the batch row.
  2. A batch must be Released before any units transfer to a sellable location.

Step 5 — Print the label (batch code on the label)

  1. Print the product label; the batch code prints on the label (ties shelf units back to the BPR + lot for recall scoping). Set Label printed? = Yes.
  2. Confirm label compliance basics (RESEARCH_FACTS B): identity on PDP, net quantity dual US+metric, name/place of business, INCI ingredients. No therapeutic/drug claims unless a monograph + Drug Facts panel exists.

Step 6 — Transfer released units to Shopify (the handoff)

  1. In the batch row, set Shopify location = the sellable location (e.g. Retail or Online/Shipping), enter Transfer qty, Transfer date, Staff initials.
  2. In Shopify, move that quantity into the same sellable location so Shopify (the inventory system of record) shows the units as available to sell. The Control Room records that the handoff happened; Shopify is the live sellable count.

Day-one Shopify locations (R-052): Online/Shipping, Retail, Production Back Room, Market A, Market B, Hold/Damaged/Tester. Production batches are made into Production Back Room and transferred out to a sellable location only after Release.

Step 7 — COGS rollup + traceability

  • Run 💵 Recompute COGS rollup to refresh COGS_Rollup (per-product avg unit COGS) — this is the figure that feeds QuickBooks.
  • Use 🔎 Trace a batch/lot to see materials-in (which lots) and complaints-out for any batch — your recall tool.

Product Claim Review SOP

This is regulatory research, not legal advice. Have counsel review labels and claims before opening. FDA is actively enforcing the cosmetic-vs-drug line (Dr. Brite warning letter, 2024). Treat every therapeutic-sounding claim as a liability until it is reviewed and marked green.

0. The one hard rule

No high-risk claim goes on shelf signage, a sales script, or POS / receipt / website copy until its row in claim_tracker.csv (and the Control Room Claim_Tracker tab) is marked green.

red and yellow claims are do-not-publish. A yellow may be sold as a plain product (no claim); it just cannot carry the contested claim until cleared. A red is do-not-sell-as-claimed.

1. The cosmetic-vs-drug line (intended use)

What makes a product a drug is intended use, not the ingredient. A claim that the product treats, cures, prevents, mitigates, or affects the structure/function of the body turns a cosmetic into an unapproved new drug — illegal regardless of whether the claim is true.

  • Tallow is not a monograph active. So any therapeutic claim on a tallow product is an illegal drug claim, full stop. There is no "but it really works" exception.
  • A genuine drug claim (e.g. pain relief) is only legal if the product is built as an OTC drug: an OTC monograph active (e.g. menthol/camphor for pain) + a Drug Facts panel + monograph compliance. That is a different, heavier path — flag it to the owner, do not improvise it.

2. How to reframe (cosmetic-safe wording)

Replace structure/function and disease words with appearance / sensory / cosmetic words.

Don't say (drug) Do say (cosmetic)
treats / heals / cures / relieves moisturizes / conditions / soothes the feel of
reduces inflammation / pain (drop entirely unless built as OTC drug)
repairs reduces the appearance of
grows lashes / increases growth conditions lashes; makes lashes look fuller/longer
treats rash / eczema moisturizes & protects skin from dryness
decongestant / clears congestion aromatic / cooling aroma
remineralizes / anticavity / antigingivitis freshens, cleanses, removes surface stains

Rule of thumb: appearance, moisturize, condition, freshen, aroma = safe. Treat, heal, grow, cure, prevent, anti-[disease] = drug.

3. Insect repellents — EPA FIFRA / 25(b) minimum-risk exemption

Repelling insects is a pesticidal claim = EPA registration UNLESS the product meets ALL conditions of the 25(b) minimum-risk exemption (40 CFR 152.25(f)):

  1. Only listed 25(b) actives — cedarwood, citronella, geraniol, lemongrass, peppermint, rosemary, thyme oils, etc. Verify each active is on the list.
  2. Only permitted inerts — edible fats/oils are eligible inerts.
  3. Full ingredient label + producer name and contact.
  4. NO disease linkage — may say "repels mosquitoes / ticks" but NOT "ticks that carry Lyme."
  5. NO safety claims (no "safe," "non-toxic," "child-safe," etc.).
  6. NO "EPA approved" / "EPA registered" language.

Tail risk: EPA is actively considering removing personal mosquito/tick repellents from 25(b). If that happens, these products would need full EPA registration. Re-verify before scaling the line.

If any condition fails, the product needs EPA registration — do not ship it as exempt.

4. Pet products — grooming-aid vs animal-drug line

  • A "grooming aid" that only cleanses/beautifies is largely unregulated by FDA. Keep it strictly cosmetic: "moisturizes / conditions paws / coat." Zero health language.
  • A health/treatment claim ("treats hot spots, heals, anti-itch") makes it an unapproved animal drug (FDA CVM).
  • A topical pet repellent with a pest claim = EPA (FIFRA / 25(b)) — same six conditions as §3, or EPA registration. Safest path: grooming spray with zero pest language.

5. The green / yellow / red workflow

For each product (rows seeded in claim_tracker.csv):

  1. Identify the claim concern — the specific contested claim (disease / pain / heal / grow / repellent / oral / eye / baby / pet / food).
  2. Classify intended use — cosmetic, drug, pesticide, animal drug, or food. Use §1, §3, §4.
  3. Set the Verdict: - red — claim is an unapproved drug/pesticide/food-path claim as written; do-not-sell-as-claimed. - yellow — sellable as a plain product, but the contested claim needs review/limiting before use. - green — reviewed, wording is cosmetic-safe (or properly built as an OTC drug), approved.
  4. Write the compliant reframe — the exact safe wording (col F of the tracker).
  5. Flag MoCRA carve-out — eye-mucous-membrane, injected, internal-use, or alters appearance >24h products lose the small-biz exemption; mark Yes/No/Check.
  6. Flag Drug Facts — Yes only if the product is intentionally built as an OTC drug.
  7. Reviewer signs + dates the row; only then may Shelf copy approved? flip to Yes.
  8. Nothing publishes until green. Shelf signage, sales scripts, POS/receipt copy, and website copy all draw from green rows only.

6. Where this lives

  • The 11 higher-risk products are pre-seeded in branches/templates/claim_tracker.csv and the Control Room Claim_Tracker tab (green/yellow/red conditional formatting).
  • The Control Room only tracks review status — it is not the authority. The authority is FDA/EPA/CA law and your reviewing counsel.

7. Day-one gate

All 11 rows must be green (claims reframed or removed, MoCRA/Drug Facts resolved) before any of those products carry a claim on the shelf, in a script, or at POS. A product may open as a plain, claim-free item while its claim review is still yellow — but the claim itself stays off the floor.

Complaints & Adverse Events SOP (runbook)

This is regulatory research, not legal advice. When in doubt about reporting obligations, contact the insurer and counsel the same day.

A complaint about a cosmetic isn't just customer service — it can be a serious adverse event with a hard 15-business-day FDA reporting clock. Treat reactions, eyes, mouths, babies, pets, and ingestions as escalations until proven otherwise.

1. Same-day escalation categories

Any of these = escalate the same day (auto-Yes on the form):

  • Rash / reaction (any skin reaction, burning, swelling)
  • Eye (irritation, pain, vision change, contact with eye)
  • Oral (mouth/gum irritation, swallowing a rinse/tooth product)
  • Baby (any complaint on a baby product or affecting an infant)
  • Pet (any reaction in an animal)
  • Ingestion (product swallowed)
  • Pain / infection (reported pain or signs of infection)
  • Medical visit (customer saw / will see a doctor, ER, urgent care, or vet)
  • Multiple complaints on one batch/lot (signals a batch problem)

2. The runbook (steps in order)

  1. Log it in the Control Room Complaints tab. Record the Shopify order ID (links to the customer) — NOT the customer's name, email, phone, or address (see §4). Capture: date reported, date purchased, product, variant, batch/lot, issue category, medical care Y/N.
  2. Flag escalation. If it hits any §1 category, set Same-day escalation? = Yes and notify the owner immediately — do not wait for end-of-day.
  3. Stop the batch if needed. If the issue points to a batch (multiple complaints, contamination, reaction pattern), put the batch on Hold in Batches (Release status -> Hold) so no more units ship.
  4. Pull / trace the lot. Use the batch lot trace (Batches.Batch_ID -> Material_Ledger) to scope which units and raw-material lots are affected. Quarantine retention samples and remaining stock.
  5. Save all comms and photos. Screenshots of messages, customer photos, and the order detail go to the access-controlled Drive folder; link them in the Photos link cell. Do not paste raw PII into the sheet.
  6. Notify the owner with: product, batch/lot, category, whether medical care is involved, and whether other complaints exist on the same batch.
  7. Contact insurer / regulatory as warranted. For any reaction involving medical care, eyes, oral, baby, ingestion, or a possible serious adverse event, notify the insurance broker/carrier the same day and consult counsel on reporting.
  8. Evaluate FDA serious-adverse-event reporting. A serious adverse event (death; life- threatening; hospitalization; disability; congenital anomaly; needing medical/surgical intervention to prevent the above; or a significant disfigurement/infection/serious reaction) must be reported by the Responsible Person to FDA within 15 BUSINESS DAYS, with the retail label attached. Start the clock at first awareness. When unsure if it qualifies, treat it as serious and consult counsel inside the window.
  9. Record retention. Keep adverse-event records 6 years (3 years for a qualifying small business). Keep the complaint row, comms, photos, batch trace, and any FDA submission together.

3. Decision shortcut

Signal Action
Reaction + medical care / ER / vet Escalate same-day; notify owner + insurer; evaluate 15-day FDA report
Eye / oral / ingestion / baby Escalate same-day; treat as potentially serious
2+ complaints, same batch Hold the batch, trace the lot, evaluate recall scope
Single mild dissatisfaction, no reaction Log, resolve as normal CS; no escalation

4. Data boundary (non-negotiable)

  • The Complaints tab stores a Shopify order ID, never the customer's name / email / phone / address. Customer PII stays in Shopify.
  • Photos and comms live in an access-controlled Drive folder, linked — not pasted into the sheet.
  • Never paste card data, SSNs, or other forbidden fields (see SECURITY.md).

5. Where this lives

  • Log: Control Room Complaints tab.
  • Intake form spec: branches/form_specs/complaint_form_spec.md.
  • Batch trace: Batches + Material_Ledger tabs.
  • The Control Room tracks complaints; FDA reporting and insurer notification happen on the real rails (FDA, the broker/carrier).
