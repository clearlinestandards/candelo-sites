“Tell me about a time you turned around an underperforming location.”
SITUATION Oakland Main Hospital Pharmacy. Two-month assignment, sent in because performance needed to
improve.
ACTION
First week was observation, not changes — where the work stalled, where staff were doubling back,
which step created the queue. Then one workflow change at a time, trained on the floor, with the
leads owning it.
RESULT
 — and it held after the assignment ended.
PILLAR
Performance turnaround.
“Walk me through a day with competing priorities.”
SITUATION Two call-outs, a system outage, a full waiter queue, and an escalated member — one morning, one of
the busiest sites in the area.
ACTION
Triage in order: safety first, then the member in front of you, then throughput. Redeployed staff to the
bottleneck, moved non-urgent work to later in the day, took the escalation yourself so the team stayed
on the queue.
RESULT
.

High-volume execution.
“Tell me about a difficult performance conversation.”
SITUATION An employee whose attendance or output was affecting the rest of the team — and the team knew it.
ACTION
Private, specific, documented. Named the behavior and the impact, not the person. Set a clear
expectation with a date, then followed up on that date instead of letting it drift.
RESULT
. Say what happened even if the answer was separation — owning that reads as
stronger, not weaker.

“Tell me about a change people resisted.”
SITUATION A workflow that had been done the same way for years and was creating the backlog.
ACTION
Brought the staff who ran it into the fix instead of announcing it. Piloted it short, measured it, showed
them their own numbers, adjusted the part they were right about.
RESULT
, and the team defended the new process to the next site.
PILLAR
Change and process improvement.
“Tell me about an escalated member complaint.”
SITUATION A member whose issue had already gone past the staff member in front of them.
ACTION
Took it off the counter, listened without defending, fixed what could be fixed that day, and told them
exactly what would happen next — then closed the loop with the staff member so it wasn't just
handled, it was taught.
RESULT
Resolved without further escalation; 
.

Member experience.

 — and the site ran without you standing in it.

A S K T H E M
Questions that make you sound like the peer, not the candidate
T R A P S
What loses the room
Prepared as an interview value package. Fill the blanks, rehearse the opening and the closer out loud,
and cap every behavioral answer at two minutes.
What does success look like in the first 90 days for whoever takes this?
Which metric is under the most pressure right now — and what's already
been tried?
How much authority does this role have to change workflow directly,
versus escalate a recommendation?
What's the widest gap between your strongest and weakest site, and
what do you think is driving it?
Who would I be partnering with — pharmacists in charge, ops, HR — and
where do those lines actually run day to day?
“I improved the metrics.” Unquantified claims read as no claim at all.
Number, timeframe, mechanism — every time.
Describing the problem without the change. They don't need the
diagnosis, they need what you did about it.
“We” for everything. Credit the team, then say the decision that was
yours. Both, in that order.
The four-minute STAR answer. Cap it at two. Let them ask for more —
that's the sign it landed.
Anything negative about a site or a leader. Say what the operation
needed, never who failed it.
